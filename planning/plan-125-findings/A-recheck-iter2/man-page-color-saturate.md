UNIT:      man-page:color/saturate
CLAIM:     "A grey does not stay grey. A colour with no saturation has no meaningful hue, but color::toHsl reports its hue as 0.0, and 0.0 degrees is red — so saturate(color::gray(128), 1.0) is very nearly pure red, not a grey."
VERDICT:   incomplete
EVIDENCE:  Probe at /tmp/plan-125-scratch/A-recheck-iter2/man-page-color-saturate/probe.mfb (built with `mfb build -q p_probe`): `saturate(rgb(255,255,255), 1.0)` printed `#ffffff`, `saturate(rgb(0,0,0), 1.0)` printed `#000000`, `saturate(gray(128), 1.0)` printed `#ff0101`, `saturate(gray(1), 1.0)` printed `#020000`, `saturate(gray(254), 1.0)` printed `#fffdfd`. The two extreme greys are the one case the flat rule gets wrong, and a reader who tests on black or white first will conclude the warning is false.
SUGGESTED: "A grey does not stay grey (black and white excepted — at lightness 0.0 or 1.0 saturation has no effect). A colour with no saturation has no meaningful hue, but color::toHsl reports its hue as 0.0, and 0.0 degrees is red — so saturate(color::gray(128), 1.0) is very nearly pure red, not a grey, and the closer a grey is to mid-lightness the redder it comes back."
