### 1. "always equals len(strings::graphemes(value))" hides that the two calls differ in fallibility
UNIT:      man-page:strings/graphemesCount
CLAIM:     "`strings::graphemesCount` returns the number of Unicode extended grapheme clusters in `value`; the result always equals `len(strings::graphemes(value))`."
VERDICT:   incomplete
EVIDENCE:  The counts agree on every probe (13 inputs, each printing `gc=` and `lenG=` identical: empty, `\r\n`, flag, ZWJ family, skin tone, Hangul jamo, Indic conjunct, keycap, astral emoji, combining e). But `mfb man strings graphemes` lists `ErrOutOfMemory` in its Errors table, while this page's descriptor (`src/codegen/builtins/strings/func_graphemes_count.rs`, `errors: vec![]`) declares none and the page says "the call never fails". The sentence is true whenever `graphemes` returns, but it is the one place the page could tell the reader why to prefer `graphemesCount` for a pure count, and the later "call `strings::graphemes` once and take `len`" advice points the reader at the fallible form without saying so. Minor, and the only thing I could fault.
SUGGESTED: "...the result always equals `len(strings::graphemes(value))` whenever that call succeeds; unlike `strings::graphemes`, which builds a list and can raise `ErrOutOfMemory`, `graphemesCount` builds nothing and never fails."

Everything else on the page checks out against the binary. Both examples were compiled as projects under `/tmp/plan-125-scratch/D-phase4-current/man-page-strings-graphemesCount/{ex1,ex2}` with the P-125 release `mfb build` and printed `3 / 3` and `3 / 4` exactly as the page implies. The probe project there (`probe/src/main.mfb`) printed, per input, `gc`, `len(graphemes)`, `len`, and `byteLen`: empty `0/0/0/0`; `\r\n` `1/1/2/2`; `a\r\nb` `3/3/4/4`; `\n\n` `2/2/2/2`; one flag `1/1/2/8`; two flags `2/2/4/16`; ZWJ family `1/1/5/18`; skin-tone `1/1/2/8`; Hangul jamo `1/1/3/9`; Indic conjunct `1/1/3/9`; `😀` `1/1/1/4`; `e\u{301}fg` `3/3/4/5`; keycap `1/1/3/7`; an `AttributedString` built with `astrings::fromString("e\u{301}fg")` returned `3`; and the source `String` printed unchanged after the call. The banned-vocabulary alternation from `scripts/man-census.sh --banned-list` grepped whole-word against the rendered page returns no hit, and no sentence teaches a borrow or ownership model in other words.
